How AquaVein-SAR Works (Scientific Basis)

A 6-stage coastal detection and utilization model grounded in NASA-ISRO NISAR, NASA/CNES SWOT, GRACE-FO, UNESCO WWDR2, and NATO SAR educational literature.

01. Multi-Orbit SAR Scanning

Using NISAR's 24 cm L-Band and 9.4 cm S-Band microwave principles (Berens, 2007), coastal regions are scanned 24/7 regardless of cloud cover or darkness.

02. Bragg Scattering & Wave Damping

Lower-density fluids rising to the sea surface damp short capillary waves, creating a lower radar backscatter signature compared to surrounding seawater.

03. SWOT & GRACE-FO Prioritization

GRACE-FO aquifer mass-change anomalies and SWOT coastal topography help narrow down active karstic coastal candidate zones.

04. Subsea Collection Dome (Concept)

A modular subsea dome is positioned over the detected freshwater vent to capture outflow before complete mixing with salty ocean water.

05. Low-Pressure Filtration

Avoiding the high osmotic pressure required in seawater reverse osmosis significantly lowers pumping and treatment energy compared to conventional SWRO plants.

06. Coastal Greenhouse Delivery

Filtered natural water is routed directly to coastal greenhouses, supporting resilient agricultural output even during inland droughts.

Blue Economy & Verified Energy Comparison

Coastal agriculture and ecosystem benefits evaluated against peer-reviewed desalination energy benchmarks.

SWRO: 3.5–4.5 kWh/m³

Peer-reviewed studies report total specific energy consumption (including pre/post-treatment) of real seawater reverse osmosis (SWRO) plants at 3.5–4.5 kWh/m³ (theoretical minimum ~1.07 kWh/m³). Capturing low-salinity natural springs aims to avoid high-pressure RO pumping.

Global Groundwater & FSGD

According to UNESCO (2006, p. 121), ~30% of Earth's freshwater resides in groundwater aquifers. Luijendijk et al. (2020) and Zhou et al. (2019) demonstrate that fresh submarine groundwater discharge (FSGD) plays a vital role in coastal water budgets.

Zero Concentrated Brine

Collecting naturally discharging low-salinity freshwater from the seabed rather than separating high-salinity seawater eliminates concentrated brine discharge into fragile coastal habitats.
